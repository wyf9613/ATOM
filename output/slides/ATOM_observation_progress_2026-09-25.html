01 · Operating-target localisation

Two assumptions give a coarse starting pose

1 · The workstation target is calibrated

The pick or place target has a known precise position and orientation in the world frame.

2 · The robot base is located approximately

A rough planar base position in the world frame is available. Its localisation method is undecided; we conservatively expect limited accuracy.

What this makes available in the robot base frame

Known accuratelyTarget height for the pre-observation pose
Known approximatelyBearing toward the target

The following workflow and simulations are conditional on these assumptions. The present simulation generates the bearing from ground truth plus ±5° noise; it does not implement mobile-base localisation.

02 · Pre-observation and approach

Three motions, with a target snapshot at each boundary

Motion 1 · Pre-observationReach the viewing pose

Subscribe to target height and coarse bearing. Place the end-effector origin 0.35 m along that bearing, match camera height to the tag, and orient the gripper horizontally toward it. Detect the requested tag.

Motion 2 · AlignmentMove to 0.40 m standoff

Freeze the selected tag pose at segment start. MoveIt plans a height- and tilt-checked trajectory while the camera remains aimed toward the estimated tag center. The front reference point ends 0.40 m from the tag plane.

Motion 3 · Perpendicular approachMove to 0.10 m standoff

Freeze the latest valid observation from Motion 2. Follow its estimated plane-normal ray to 0.10 m, holding end-effector height, roll and pitch; check camera pointing and the achieved pose.

Continuous observation stream during Motions 2 and 3 → topic + log

Process the selected tag at up to 5 Hz; transform each valid pose into link_base. Updates during a motion are published and recorded, but do not change its executing trajectory.

Segment boundary

Motion 2 uses the pre-observation result. Motion 3 uses the newest valid Motion 2 result. Motion 3 updates are recorded for evaluation only.

MoveIt plans each segment; sampled FK checks height, tilt, camera pointing and the perpendicular corridor before execution.

Current updates are published continuously. Later work can reuse this observation node for obstacle mapping and path planning; environment obstacle avoidance and online replanning are not yet implemented.

Repeated RGB test: the team's separate 10-run experiment observed 4 terminations due to planning failure (4/10). Under different viewing angles, RGB pose-estimation error can affect planning; the exact causal breakdown remains to be checked against those run logs. The single-run comparison above shows slightly lower overall terminal XY error for RGB-D, but the seeds differ and one success per mode does not establish a precision advantage. Both modes currently estimate tag pose with RGB AprilTag PnP; depth is recorded for quality, not fused into pose. Single-run analysis and raw-data paths.

04 · Next steps

From visual approach to a physical transfer

1Add arm-reach constraints to observation

Check reachability while selecting the pre-observation pose, so the requested view can be planned reliably.

2Integrate the gripper control code

Obtain the inherited implementation and complete the tag-guided pick and place sequence, including grasp and release feedback.

3Build and test the fixture

Design and 3D-print the tube rack and camera adapter, measure their geometry and extrinsics, then run a small supervised hardware experiment.

Near-term acceptance: a measured, repeatable slot-1 → slot-2 transfer with logged pose error and explicit failure reasons.
